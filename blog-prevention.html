Screenings worth booking in your 30s and 40s

The checkups that catch problems while they're still easy to treat.

FA
Dr. Femi Adeyemi
5 min read Jun 5, 2026
Share

Most of the conditions that become serious in your 50s and 60s were quietly detectable a decade or two earlier — which is exactly what screenings are for. None of these require symptoms to be worth booking. Here's what tends to matter most in your 30s and 40s, and why.

Starting in your 30s

Blood pressure and cholesterol, as a baseline

Both can drift upward for years without any symptoms at all, which is exactly why they're worth checking on a schedule rather than waiting to feel unwell. A baseline reading in your 30s also gives your provider something to compare future results against, making small changes easier to catch.

Annually, from your 20s on

A skin check, even without a worrying spot

Skin cancer is one of the more treatable cancers when caught early, and a lot of concerning changes are easy to miss on your own — a spot on your back, behind an ear, on your scalp. An annual look from a dermatologist, or even a careful self-check with a mirror, catches things that are easy to overlook otherwise.

"The screenings people skip aren't usually the scary ones. They're the boring ones — the ones that don't feel urgent until the year they suddenly are."
Every year or two

Dental and vision checks, not just for problems

These get treated as optional far more than they should. Gum disease and vision changes both progress quietly, and both are linked to broader health issues — gum inflammation to heart health, vision changes to diabetes risk — that a routine check can flag well before either becomes obvious on its own.

Entering your 40s

The screenings that typically start here

This is usually when a provider starts discussing additional screenings — for certain cancers, diabetes, and other age-related risks — timed to when they become statistically more relevant. Exactly which ones and when depends heavily on your personal and family history, which is why this is a conversation to have directly with your doctor rather than a fixed checklist to follow alone.

A note from the clinic

Screening schedules vary by country, personal risk factors, and family history, so treat this as a general map rather than a personal one. Bring up your family's health history at your next visit — it often changes which screenings make sense, and when.

None of these screenings are exciting, which is precisely why they're easy to keep postponing. But the entire value of a screening is catching something while it's still small and manageable — which only works if it's booked before there's a reason to feel worried.
